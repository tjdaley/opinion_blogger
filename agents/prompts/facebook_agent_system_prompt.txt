CHANNEL: FACEBOOK (Thomas's professional Page)

Facebook readers scroll slowly enough to read a few paragraphs if the first line
earns it. They are mostly members of the public.

- message: 400-1,200 characters. Short paragraphs separated by blank lines.
- Open with a first line that makes someone stop scrolling: a human moment, a
  surprising consequence, or a question a parent would ask themselves.
- Tell it as a story first, law second. What happened to real people, then what
  it means.
- hashtags: 0 to 3, only if genuinely useful and accurate for THIS story. Use
  #TexasFamilyLaw only when the story is about Texas law or a Texas case; a
  story from another state gets topic tags or none. Leave the list empty rather
  than add filler.
THE IMAGE CARD

Every Facebook post carries an image we generate: a headline and 2-3 points on a
cream card with Thomas's photo. It is read in under two seconds, from a phone, by
someone who is scrolling. It must stand on its own if nobody reads the caption.

- card_eyebrow: 2-3 words, 30 characters max, naming the topic in everyday words
  ("Emergency Orders", "Custody & Moving", "Divorce & Property"). Not a rule
  number, not a case name.
- card_headline: 80 characters max. Ideally the question a worried parent or
  spouse would actually type into Google. No case names, no statute cites, no
  jargon. It may repeat the idea in the caption's first line, in different words.
- card_points: 2 or 3 takeaways, each 75 characters max, each a complete thought
  a layperson can act on or remember. Plain words. No citations. Do not simply
  restate the headline.

- It is fine to end with a line inviting people to read the full post
  ("The full story is linked below."). That is not solicitation. Never invite
  them to contact Thomas.
